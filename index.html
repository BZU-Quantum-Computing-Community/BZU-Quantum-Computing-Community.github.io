Open the black box

Meet the qubit.

Your laptop thinks in bits — each one is a 0 or a 1, nothing in between. A quantum computer thinks in qubits, and a qubit can lean toward 0 and 1 at the same time. That one strange fact is where all the power comes from.

This sphere is the qubit's home: the north pole means 0, the south pole means 1, and everywhere in between is superposition. Drag it to look around.

Press the H gate to swing the arrow into superposition, then Measure — and watch reality pick an answer.

In the series you'll write the same experiment in Python and run it on IBM's real quantum processors — machines kept colder than outer space.

FAQ

Frequently asked

Do I need to know quantum physics or coding?
No quantum background needed — the series starts from zero. A little Python helps but isn't required: our team walks you through the setup and everything after it, step by step, in the room.
Can I come to the Open Day without registering?
Yes! The whole Open Day on 10 October is open to every student on campus — booths, games and the guest speaker — with no registration needed. Registering is for the four working sessions that follow, so sign up if you want the full series.
What should I bring?
For the Open Day, just curiosity. For the four working sessions, bring your laptop — everything there is hands-on.
What exactly is Qiskit?
Qiskit is IBM's open-source toolkit for programming quantum computers in Python. The series follows IBM's official "Use a Quantum Computer Today" course — but instead of watching it alone, we do it together, in person, as part of Qiskit Fall Fest.

Curiosity is the only requirement.

No physics background. No coding experience. If you've ever wondered what's really inside a quantum computer, this series was made for you.
